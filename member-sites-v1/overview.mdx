A member site is a static web app, such as a Vite and React app, that runs on your company's storefront. Members open it at an address under `/sites/`, inside the storefront's own cart, sign-in and account menu. You build it like any static app, and you publish its build output with the Fluid CLI.


<Note>
  Fluid turns member sites on for your company. Until they are on, publishing answers `404`. To ask for them, [contact support](/help/getting-help).
</Note>

## How a member site runs

A visitor opens a site at its storefront address:

```text
https://yoli.com/sites/diamond-leaders
https://yoli.com/sites/diamond-leaders/events
```

`diamond-leaders` is the site's handle: lowercase letters, digits and single hyphens, at most 40 characters, unique in your company. Everything after the handle is the site's own path. The storefront API calls the handle the site's `slug`.

Sites used to open under `/accounts/`. Those addresses no longer open a site, and they don't redirect, so update any link that still uses one. Account screens such as `/accounts/profile` aren't sites and keep their addresses.

The storefront draws the page around your site:

- **The storefront's own parts.** The cart drawer, checkout, sign-in and the account menu belong to the storefront. Your site uses them through the SDK, so member pricing and the visit's credit are the storefront's own. A signed-in member sees the account menu's button in the corner. A guest sees the storefront's lead-capture widget there instead, with no sign-in button, so give guests your own with `fluid.auth.signIn()`.
- **One frame for your site.** Your site runs in one full-page frame, served from its own host under `sites.fluid.app`. The frame is sandboxed, so your code can't move the storefront's window by script. Link to storefront pages with plain `<a href>` links, and the SDK opens them in the storefront's window when the visitor clicks. See [Link to the storefront](/member-sites-v1/sdk#link-to-the-storefront).
- **One address bar.** The storefront keeps the address bar and the history. Back and Forward move through your site's pages, one entry per page.
- **Page titles and previews.** The storefront writes each page's title, link preview and canonical link from your site's `config/meta.json`. A value your file leaves out comes from the site's SEO settings, then its title, description and image, then your store's defaults. See [Page titles and previews](/member-sites-v1/declarations#page-titles-and-previews) and [SEO and the page head](/storefront/sites#seo-and-the-page-head).

If someone opens your site's own host directly, they land on its storefront address, so your site always opens inside the storefront.

## Who can open a site

A site belongs to a member or to the company. A new site is private until its owner makes it public.

| Visibility | Who can open it |
| --- | --- |
| `public` | Anyone, signed in or not |
| `private` | The owner, company admins, and members a share admits |

A site that's switched off (`active: false`) opens only for its owner and company admins, whatever its visibility. See [Switch a site off](/storefront/sites#switch-a-site-off).

Sites are shared within one company. A visitor who isn't a member of your company is a guest, and a guest opens public sites only. A signed-in member a private site doesn't admit sees a refusal page. A guest is asked to sign in.

## Share a site

A published site has a link at its storefront address, such as `https://yoli.com/sites/diamond-leaders`. A visit through that plain link credits the site's owner, or the company for a company site. A member's own share link, the site's `share_url` when the member reads the site through the Member API, carries their credit, such as `https://yoli.com/janedoe/sites/diamond-leaders`, so the member who shared it gets credit for what the visitor buys. A credited `/<credit>/sites/<handle>` link opens the site only when your company is on the sites gateway.

**Copy link** copies the site's plain link. You find it in Mist's preview pane and in the **Shares** panel of the site's screen in the Fluid admin. It shows once the site is published.

### Who manages what

| Task | Who does it | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Make a site public or private | The owner. For a company site, a company admin | `fluid sites visibility public` or `private`, with the owner's login. A company admin can also change a company site's **Visibility** in the Fluid admin |
| Choose who opens a private site | The owner shares a member's site with a member, their downline, or a segment by member type and country. A company admin shares a company site with a member or a segment | **Share** in Mist's preview pane, for a company site. Or [Share a site](/api-reference/site-shares/share-a-site) and [Share a company site](/api-reference/member-site-shares/share-a-company-site) |
| See and remove a site's shares | A company admin | The site's **Shares** panel in the Fluid admin |
| Take a site down | A company admin | **Take down** on the site's screen in the Fluid admin, or the API. Both take an optional reason. Its owner can't publish, schedule, switch on or make public the site until an admin brings it back, but can still edit it. See [When a site is taken down](/storefront/sites#when-a-site-is-taken-down) |
| Bring a site back | A company admin | **Bring back** on the site's screen in the Fluid admin |

In the Fluid admin, the sites are under **Website** > **Sites**. Open a site to see its details, its shares and its actions. The admin doesn't add shares. Share a company site from Mist, or through the API.

## Company sites on the storefront

A site your company owns is also a storefront resource, like a page or a product. It has SEO, metafields, countries, a `draft`, `scheduled`, `published` or `archived` status and an on/off switch, and the public storefront API lists it once it's live. Member-owned sites are never in that list. See [Company sites on the storefront](/storefront/sites).

## Start from the template

Every new member site starts from the member site template, a blank Vite and React app. It builds and publishes as it is, and it has:

- one home page, and a not-found page for any other path;
- a small router that follows the storefront's Back and Forward;
- CSS tokens at the top of `src/styles.css`, the only place a color or size is named;
- `config/meta.json` with the home page's title and description;
- `config/access.json` with no scopes.

It has no shop and no `db/` folder, so a new site has no database until you add a table. See [The member site database](/member-sites-v1/database).

```text
diamond-leaders/
  .gitignore
  README.md
  index.html
  package.json
  tsconfig.json
  vite.config.ts
  src/
    main.tsx            imports the SDK once
    App.tsx             the pages, by path
    Link.tsx            a link to another page of the site
    router.ts           a small router that follows Back and Forward
    styles.css          the CSS tokens
    pages/
      Home.tsx          the home page
      NotFound.tsx      the page for any other path
  config/
    access.json         the Fluid API scopes the site asks for: none
    meta.json           the home page's title and description
```

To add a page, add a component in `src/pages/`, add it to the pages in `src/App.tsx`, link to it with `<Link>`, and give it a title in `config/meta.json`.

`db/schema.json` and the files in `config/` are your site's declarations. They're published with the build and never served as files. Each is optional: leave one out and the site gets its default.

| File | What it declares | Page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `db/schema.json` | The site's tables, their columns and who may read and write them | [Database](/member-sites-v1/database) |
| `config/access.json` | The Fluid API scopes and member fields the site uses | [Declarations](/member-sites-v1/declarations#fluid-data-the-site-uses) |
| `config/meta.json` | Each page's title, description and preview image | [Declarations](/member-sites-v1/declarations#page-titles-and-previews) |
| `config/routes.json` | Which file answers each path | [Declarations](/member-sites-v1/declarations#routes) |

## Build in Mist

When member sites are turned on for your company, Mist shows a **Member sites** section in its sidebar, with a **New member site** button. Name the site, and Mist shows the handle the name becomes. Mist creates a company site from the blank template and opens it.

**Run** starts the site in the preview pane with `fluid sites dev`, on a private local database. Its rows stay on your computer, apart from the live site's. When a change to `db/schema.json` would delete local data, the preview shows the change with an **Apply and restart** button and waits for you. See [Develop locally](/member-sites-v1/publishing#develop-locally).

The site's preview pane has these buttons:

- **Publish** builds the site and publishes it. When a change to `db/schema.json` would delete data, Mist lists each step in plain words and asks before it applies them. Nothing is published until you choose **Apply and publish**.
- **Copy link** copies the site's link. See [Share a site](#share-a-site).
- **Share**, on a company site, opens its sharing settings:
  - **Invite people**: find a member by name or email and give them **Can view** or **Can copy**.
  - **People with access**: change or remove each share.
  - **General access**: switch between **Public** and **Private**. While the site is private, **Add people who match** shares it with everyone of a member type, a country, or both.
  - **Admins only**: turn on **Pin this site for everyone** to list it first for everyone it's shared with, without letting them remove it.
  - **Visit** opens the site and **Copy link** copies its link. Both work once the site is published.

You can also ask Mist in chat to create a site, or to grow one: add pages, a shop with add to cart and checkout, storefront links that keep the visit's credit, a database table, or content for signed-in members.

## Build and publish

Install the dependencies, build, check and publish:

```bash
npm install
npm run build            # typechecks, then writes dist/
fluid sites lint         # checks dist/, db/ and config/
fluid sites publish      # uploads what changed, and makes it live
```

A folder Mist didn't create needs its site first: run `fluid sites create` in it. See [Before you start](/member-sites-v1/publishing#before-you-start).

To try the site before you publish it, run `fluid sites dev`. It serves the site at `http://127.0.0.1:9500` with a private local database, so `fluid.db` works. The cart, sign-in and the Fluid API answer only once the site is published and opened at its storefront address. See [Develop locally](/member-sites-v1/publishing#develop-locally).

[Publish a member site](/member-sites-v1/publishing) covers the login, the checks, and what each answer means. For a direct integration, see [Member Sites APIs](/api/member-sites).

## Use the SDK

Your site talks to Fluid through `@fluid-app/sites`. Import it once, at the top of your app:

```ts src/main.tsx
import "@fluid-app/sites";
```

Then use `fluid` anywhere:

```ts
import { fluid } from "@fluid-app/sites";

const me = await fluid.auth.me();
await fluid.cart.add([{ variant_id: 48213, quantity: 1 }]);
const { data, error } = await fluid.db.from("rsvps").select("name,guests");
```

`fluid.db` answers once the site has a database. [The member sites SDK](/member-sites-v1/sdk) covers each part.

## What member sites don't do yet

- **No server functions.** A site is static files and the SDK. `fluid.fn.fetch` answers `404`, and `fluid sites publish` refuses a site with a `functions/` folder or `config/functions.json`.
- **No service workers.** The site's host refuses a service worker's script.
- **No files under `/_fluid/`.** The site's host answers that path itself, and publishing refuses a build with a file under it.
- **No SQL from the browser.** Your code reads and writes its tables through `fluid.db`, and the site's table rules decide each request.
- **No secrets in the site.** Everything in your build is public to whoever can open the site. Don't put an API key or a token in it.
- **No visitors from other companies.** A person who isn't a member of your company opens your sites as a guest.
