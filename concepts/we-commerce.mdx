We-Commerce is the philosophy at the heart of Fluid: a model where merchants and sales representatives work together on a single platform, with every sale, attribution, and commission fully visible to all parties. Unlike traditional e-commerce — where the storefront and the sales team operate in separate systems — We-Commerce treats reps as first-class participants in every transaction, from the first click to the final payout.

Fluid doesn't calculate commissions. If you already run a commission engine, you connect its data to Fluid and keep it. Fluid gives you what commission engines lack: modern storefronts, checkout, payments, and social, agentic, and international commerce. See [Works with your commission engine](#works-with-your-commission-engine).

## The Problem with Traditional E-Commerce

Standard e-commerce platforms were built for a world where a single brand sells directly to consumers. They have no concept of a rep who referred a buyer, a commission that needs to flow back to a distributor, or attribution that spans multiple websites. The moment you introduce direct sales into the picture, you're forced to stitch together spreadsheets, third-party affiliate tools, and manual reconciliation processes that break constantly and create disputes between merchants and reps.

We-Commerce solves this by building attribution and rep workflows directly into the platform, and by connecting to the commission engine that pays your reps — not as an afterthought, but as a core architectural concern.

## Key Problems We-Commerce Solves

**Attribution** — Knowing which rep drove a sale is harder than it sounds. Buyers visit multiple sites, clear cookies, and use different devices. Fluid's [FairShare](/concepts/fair-share) SDK tracks visitors using a layered approach — cookies, browser fingerprints, and URL-based routes — so the rep whose link a buyer followed keeps the credit across later pages, visits, and domains in the same browser. See [How credit is established and kept](/concepts/fair-share#how-credit-is-established-and-kept).

**Commission sync** — Attribution is resolved at the moment of checkout, so each order already names the rep who drove it when it syncs to your commission engine. There is no reconciliation step and no room for disputes about who drove the sale. See [Works with your commission engine](#works-with-your-commission-engine).

**Checkout** — A checkout that doesn't understand reps will strip attribution data and break the entire model. Fluid's [checkout](/concepts/checkout), hosted at `checkout.fluid.app`, is built with rep attribution as a first-class concern. FairShare data flows directly into every order placed through it.

**Payments** — Reaching global customers means accepting the payment methods they actually use. Fluid Payments supports 300+ alternative payment methods across 100+ countries, so reps can sell to buyers anywhere without hitting payment walls.

## How the Pieces Fit Together

Every component of the Fluid platform is designed to work together as a coherent whole:

- **FairShare** — the JavaScript SDK that runs on your storefront (and any external site) to capture attribution and serve the cart widget, lead capture, and media embeds.
- **Checkout** — the hosted one-page checkout that resolves attribution, calculates shipping and tax, validates addresses, and processes payments.
- **Connect** — the API layer that links your product catalog, inventory, and order data to the Fluid platform so everything stays in sync. Fluid Connect supports integrations with Exigo, ByDesign, Pillars, InfoTrax, and Shopify out of the box.
- **Payments** — the payment processing layer that handles global alternative payment methods.

Together, these components create a closed loop: a rep shares a link, FairShare captures the attribution, the buyer completes checkout, and the order syncs to your commission engine — all without manual intervention.

## Works with your commission engine

Fluid doesn't calculate commissions. If you already run a commission engine such as Exigo, InfoTrax, or Pillars, you don't have to replace it to move to Fluid. Connect its data to Fluid, and it keeps calculating and paying commissions on your compensation plan.

Commission engines are built to pay commissions. They don't have what modern ecommerce needs: agentic commerce, social commerce, international commerce, and checkout and payments that work in every market. Fluid does. You compete on the front end with Fluid, and keep your compensation plan where it is.

| Fluid runs | Your commission engine runs |
| --- | --- |
| Storefront, rep sites, and the mobile app | Commission calculation and payouts |
| Checkout and payments in 100+ countries | Your compensation plan |
| FairShare attribution: which rep earns each sale | Genealogy |
| Social and marketplace sales channels | The system of record for members, orders, and genealogy |

Fluid Connect is the integration between the two. Records sync both ways on a schedule, and Fluid writes the orders it takes back to the engine, so the engine pays commission on every sale. You keep your compensation plan exactly where it is.

## Build for your field force

If you support a field force of reps or agents, Fluid is where you build for them. [Sites](/sites/overview) give them a signed-in experience on your own storefront, and an AI agent can build it exactly the way you want. Fluid's data and connections are already inside: a member signed in on your storefront is signed in on the Site, the Site calls Fluid as that member through the [Member API](/sites/member-api), and every link they share credits them.

Sites are one of four ways to build on Fluid:

- **[Sites](/sites/overview)** — signed-in pages, dashboards, and tools for members, published with the Fluid CLI, Mist, or an AI agent.
- **[Mist apps](/mist-apps/overview)** — hosted web apps with their own server and database, built by describing them to Mist.
- **[Themes](/themes/overview)** — your storefront and member storefront.
- **[Widgets](/portal-widgets/overview)** — your own widgets inside the rep portal.

Fluid hosts all of them, so you can build for the field without touching your core infrastructure, including your commission engine.
