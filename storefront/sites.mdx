A company site is a [Site](/sites/overview) that your company owns, such as a launch microsite or a landing page. Besides opening at its own address, a company site is a storefront resource: it has the same common fields, lifecycle, SEO, metafields and countries as a page or a product, a public catalog, and company endpoints to manage it.

Member-owned sites aren't storefront resources. They stay on the [Member API](/api/member-sites) and are never listed in the public catalog. A member's public sites are listed on their own page instead, at `/<credit>/sites` on your store domain, and by [a member's public sites](/api-reference/member-sites/a-members-public-sites) request.

The site's pages still come from the build you publish with the Fluid CLI. See [Publish a Site](/sites/publishing). The fields on this page describe the site on the storefront.

## List your sites on the storefront

`GET /api/v202604/sites` lists your live company sites. A site is live when it's company-owned, public, switched on, not taken down, and published, or scheduled with its time passed. Call it on the store's own host, with no token:

```bash
curl "https://acme.fluid.app/api/v202604/sites?filter[country]=US&page[limit]=10"
```

The list takes the same controls as every storefront list: `q`, `sort`, `lang`, `filter[country]`, `filter[language]`, `page[cursor]` and `page[limit]`. See [Find and create resources](/storefront/find-and-create).

`GET /api/v202604/sites/{slug}` looks up one site:

```bash
curl "https://acme.fluid.app/api/v202604/sites/spring-launch"
```

A public draft or scheduled site still resolves by slug, with `seo.indexable` set to `false`. A private, archived, switched-off, taken-down or member-owned site returns `404`. See [Visibility, drafts, and scheduling](/storefront/visibility).

While Sites are off for your company, the list is empty and every lookup returns `404`.

See [Public Sites catalog](/api-reference/sites/public-sites-catalog) and [Public Site by slug](/api-reference/sites/public-site-by-slug) for the parameters and the response.

## Manage your sites

Manage company sites at `/api/v202604/company/sites` on `https://api.fluid.app`. Reads need a Bearer token with `storefront.view`. Creating, changing and deleting a site needs a signed-in admin's token with `storefront.update`: a company API token on its own can't change a site.

Writes wrap the body in a `site` key:

```bash
curl -X POST "https://api.fluid.app/api/v202604/company/sites" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer <token>" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "site": {
      "title": "Spring Launch",
      "slug": "spring-launch",
      "description": "Meet the new spring line and the people behind it.",
      "visibility": "public",
      "status": "draft",
      "country_isos": ["US", "CA"],
      "search_engine_optimizer_attributes": {
        "title": "Spring Launch | Acme Wellness"
      }
    }
  }'
```

A site takes the same common fields as a page or a product: `title`, `slug`, `description`, `image_url`, `active`, `status`, `publish_at`, SEO, metafields and countries. `status` is `draft`, `scheduled`, `published` or `archived`. `visibility` stays a site's own field: `public` lets anyone open the site, and `private` limits it to the people its shares admit.

A few things differ from pages:

- **The slug is the site's address.** Leave `slug` blank on create and Fluid proposes one from the title. Renaming a site never changes its slug.
- **Shares mean share-link statistics.** `GET /api/v202604/company/sites/{id}/shares` returns the visits that came through the site's tracked share links, as it does for a page. Who may open a private site is set through the [Member Sites APIs](/api/member-sites).
- **Scans run on every save.** Each create and update queues a Lighthouse scan and a compliance scan. Read the results at `/api/v202604/company/sites/{id}/lighthouse` and `/api/v202604/company/sites/{id}/compliance`.

The company list leaves out archived sites unless you ask for them with `filter[status]`. It also takes `filter[active]`, and `filter[slug]` for one exact slug. Member-owned sites never appear in it.

In the Fluid admin, a company site's screen sets the same fields: the **Status** card sets the status and a scheduled date, the **SEO And Link Sharing** card sets the search engine title, description, image and indexing, and **Actions** has **Archive** or **Restore**. The admin doesn't change `active`.

See the [Sites reference](/api-reference/sites/company-sites-index) for every company request, its fields and its responses.

## SEO and the page head

You don't have to write SEO tags in your site. Search engines and link previews read the storefront page around your site, and Fluid writes its head for you: the title, the description, the preview image, the Open Graph tags, a Twitter card, and the canonical link.

Each value comes from the first source that has one:

1. The page's own entry in your site's `config/meta.json`: `routeMeta`, then `pages`, for the page's exact path.
2. The site's SEO settings, which you write with `search_engine_optimizer_attributes`.
3. The `default` in your site's `config/meta.json`.
4. The site's own `title`, `description` and `image_url`.
5. Your store's defaults.

Fluid picks each value on its own, so a page that names only a title in `config/meta.json` still gets the site's description and image. The SEO settings win over the `default` in `config/meta.json`, so what an admin sets shows on every page your code doesn't name. See [SEO and indexing](/sites/seo) and [SEO for storefront resources](/storefront/seo).

A site's `canonical_url` is `/home/sites/<slug>` on your store domain, such as `https://acme.fluid.app/home/sites/spring-launch`, like every storefront resource. It takes the visitor to the site. In the page head, a page other than the site's home adds its own path to that address for the canonical link, such as `/home/sites/spring-launch/events`. Your store's `sitemap-sites.xml` lists the live company sites at those addresses.

Search engines may index a site only when it's a live, public, switched-on company site, its SEO settings have `indexable: true`, and your storefront allows indexing. A new site starts with indexing off, so turn it on when the site is ready. Every other site, every member's site, and every refusal or sign-in page tells search engines not to index it. `config/meta.json` can't turn indexing on.

## Link members to their sites

`/account` on your store domain, such as `https://acme.fluid.app/account`, takes a member to their sites. Use it in a menu, an email or a text. It needs no rep in the address.

- A visitor who isn't signed in is asked to sign in, then comes back to `/account`.
- A member with one site goes straight to it.
- A member with several sites sees them all as tiles. The site they chose as home comes first.
- A member with no site yet sees a short message saying so.

The page lists only sites the member can open now. Each one is a company site shared with them, a site they built, or a site another member shared with them. `/account` works only on a store that has Sites turned on.

## Switch a site off

`active` is a site's on/off switch, separate from `status` and from `visibility`. Set `"active": false` to take a site off the storefront without unpublishing it:

- Its owner and your company's admins can still open it.
- A guest is asked to sign in, and any other member sees not found.
- It leaves the public catalog, and its public lookup returns `404`.

Set `"active": true` to bring it back.

## When a site is taken down

A company admin can take any site down, a company site or a member's: **Take down** on the site's screen in the Fluid admin, or the [take-down request](/api-reference/member-sites/take-a-member-site-down) of the company member-site API. Both take an optional reason, at most 1000 characters. The request sends it as `reason`.

A take-down is separate from the site's `status`. While a site is down:

- Its address no longer opens it, and it's in no public catalog or lookup.
- Its owner can't publish it, schedule it, switch it on or make it public. Each of those changes is refused with `422`.
- Its owner can still edit it, such as its title, description, SEO and metafields, and can move it to draft or archive it, so they can fix what led to the take-down.

The owner is told once, in Flow: a direct message from Fluid that names the site and gives the reason. For a company site, your company's admins get the message. The reason also shows on the site. Your admins see it on the site's screen in the Fluid admin, as `taken_down_reason` on the v2026-10 company member-site API, and as `suspension_reason`, with `suspended_at`, on the v2026-04 company endpoints. A member who owns the site sees it as `taken_down_reason` when they read their site through the Member API. The public endpoints never show it.

Only a company admin can bring a site back, with **Bring back** in the Fluid admin or the [reinstate request](/api-reference/member-sites/bring-back-a-member-site-that-was-taken-down) of the company member-site API. Nothing the owner writes lifts a take-down.

For a company site, use these v2026-04 endpoints to list, create, read, change and delete it. The older company member-site API is only for what they don't cover, such as keys, environment variables, bundles and take-downs.
