Blackout dates keep subscription renewals off the days of the month you choose. For example, you can block the 29th, 30th and 31st and move those renewals to the 28th. Fluid then keeps every monthly and yearly renewal off the blocked days, for new subscriptions and for the ones you already have.

Blackout dates is a [Subscription Pro](/help/admin/settings/subscription-pro) feature.

## Before you start

- Subscription Pro is active for your company.
- If you use a blackout dates droplet, contact [Fluid support](/help/getting-help) before you turn this feature on. The droplet must be removed first, so that the two don't both change your dates.

## Where to find it

Open [Subscription Pro](/help/admin/settings/subscription-pro). On the **Settings** tab, the **Blackout dates** switch is the last row of the **Features** card. Your rules are in the **Blackout dates** section, below the **Options** card.

## How blackout dates work

### Dates Fluid works out

Fluid works out a renewal date when a subscription starts at checkout, at every renewal, when an order is skipped, when a subscription resumes without a chosen date, and when a customer pauses for a number of orders. A pause for a number of orders can therefore end on your move-to day instead of the day the pause dialog showed. The customer then sees "Your next order is" with the saved date. If that date falls on a blocked day, Fluid uses your move-to day in the same month instead.

- If the move-to day is past the end of a short month, Fluid uses the month's last day.
- A date never moves to a day before the one it is worked out from. In that case it moves to your move-to day in the next month.
- A ship date that falls on a blocked day moves the same way.
- Fluid reads the day in the subscription's own time zone.

### Dates someone picks

What happens to a chosen date on a blocked day depends on who chooses it.

**Customers.** Fluid never refuses a customer's date for a blocked day. It saves the first day on or after the chosen day that no rule blocks. It never saves an earlier day, so a customer is never charged before the day they asked for. For example, with a rule that blocks the 29th, 30th and 31st, a customer who chooses Nov 30 gets Dec 1. This applies on the customer's subscription page, in the customer portal and in the cancel flow.

- When the saved date differs from the chosen one, the customer sees "Your next order is" and the saved date, in their own date format and language.
- If the saved date would be more than 12 months away (2 years for a yearly plan), the date is refused with the usual date limit message.
- Subscriptions that renew together, in a bundle or an autoship profile, all move to the same day.

**You and your integrations.** When you choose a date in the admin, or an integration sends one with your company's credentials, Fluid doesn't save a date on a blocked day. The date stays as it was, and the message names the nearest allowed date, for example:

> 2026-11-30 falls on a blackout day. The nearest allowed date is 2026-11-28.

The nearest allowed date comes from your rule's move-to day, so it can be earlier than the chosen day.

**For everyone:**

- Date pickers in the admin, on the customer's subscription page and in the customer portal grey out the blocked days. This includes the date pickers for a bundle of subscriptions on the customer's subscriptions page and in the customer portal. The page that a renewal reminder's manage link opens has no date picker.
- In the admin, the picker also says which days of the month are blocked.
- A date someone chooses that is the subscription's current date is never moved or refused, even if a rule now blocks it. The move after you save your rules still moves it; see [What happens to existing subscriptions](#what-happens-to-existing-subscriptions).
- Admins can't override a blocked day. To allow a day, change the rule.
- A cancel offer to delay or pause the next order whose new date falls on a blocked day shows the first open day after it instead. If the customer accepts, that day is saved.

### Only monthly and yearly plans

Blackout dates apply to plans that renew every month or every year, or every few months or years. Plans that renew every few days or weeks keep their dates, and customers can pick any day for them. The one exception is a bundle: when a customer sets one date for a bundle that also holds a monthly or yearly subscription, every subscription in it moves to the same open day.

## Create a rule

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Blackout dates section">
    Open [Subscription Pro](/help/admin/settings/subscription-pro). On the **Settings** tab, find the **Blackout dates** section below the **Options** card.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a rule and choose the blocked days">
    Click **Add rule**. Under **Blocked days**, select the days of the month that no renewal may fall on.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the move-to day">
    Under **Move-to day**, choose the day that renewals on a blocked day move to. You can also give the rule a **Name**, a **Priority** and a **Checkout message**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn on Blackout dates">
    In the **Features** card, turn on **Blackout dates**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save** at the bottom of the page. The **Blackout dates** section has no save button of its own. Your rules save together with the **Features** and **Options** cards.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    The **Save blackout dates** dialog shows how many of your existing subscriptions the rules move, for example "38 subscriptions move from the 30th to the 28th." Click **Confirm and save**. Fluid then moves those subscriptions in the background.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Frame caption="A rule that blocks the 29th, 30th and 31st and moves renewals to the 28th.">
  <img src="/images/help/subscription-pro/blackout-rules.png" alt="The Blackout dates section with a rule named End of month that blocks the 29th, 30th and 31st, moves renewals to the 28th and has a checkout message" />
</Frame>

- The dialog shows only when **Blackout dates** is on after you save, the save changes the rules or turns the feature on, and at least one rule that is enabled is in force.
- If no subscription moves, the dialog says **No subscription moves.** If Fluid can't count them, the dialog says so. The count never stops you from saving.
- To go back without saving, click **Cancel** in the dialog.
- You can save rules while the switch is off. No dialog shows, and the rules do nothing until you turn the switch on. When you turn it on and save, the dialog shows.

### Rule options

- **Name**: a label for you. It appears in the note on each subscription the rule moves.
- **Blocked days**: the days of the month no renewal may fall on.
- **Move-to day**: the day a renewal on a blocked day moves to.
- **Priority**: when two rules block the same day, the rule with the higher priority wins. On a tie, the rule higher in the list wins.
- The switch on each rule: a rule that isn't switched on is kept but blocks nothing.
- **Checkout message**: text customers see at checkout when the first renewal of a new subscription was moved. Leave it empty to show nothing.

### Rule limits

- You can have up to 20 rules.
- A rule blocks from 1 to 30 days, each from the 1st to the 31st.
- The move-to day can't be a day that any rule blocks, including rules that aren't switched on.
- A move-to day after the 28th needs every day from the 28th up to it open in every rule, because in a short month it becomes the month's last day. For example, a rule that moves to the 31st can't sit next to a rule that blocks the 30th.
- A name is up to 80 characters, and a checkout message up to 500 characters.
- Priority is a whole number from -1000 to 1000. The default is 0.
- Fluid shows a problem with a rule under its field, before you save.
- If someone else saved the settings while you worked, Fluid drops your unsaved changes, reloads the settings and says that someone else changed them. Make your change again.

## What happens to existing subscriptions

When you turn on **Blackout dates**, or change your rules while it's on, and save, Fluid moves your existing subscriptions whose next renewal falls on a blocked day. The save finishes first. Fluid moves the subscriptions in the background after it.

- It moves only active and paused subscriptions on monthly and yearly plans.
- It doesn't move a subscription whose billing you switched off. It moves it after you switch billing on again.
- It leaves a renewal alone when it is due within your reminder lead time (the larger of **Reminder lead time (days)** and 3 days). A reminder may already have told the customer that date. That renewal happens on its date, and the next one moves.
- It never moves a renewal to a date inside that lead time. If your move-to day is too close, the renewal moves to your move-to day in the next month.
- It moves your subscriptions in batches of up to 200 a minute, then checks again once a day. If you save again while a move is running, a second run starts beside it, so the batches can come faster for a while. No subscription moves twice.
- A move keeps the subscription's billing day. See [The billing day](#the-billing-day).
- Each moved subscription gets a note that names the old date, the new date and the rule. For example: "Bill date moved from Nov 30, 2026 to Nov 28, 2026 by blackout rule End of month". A rule with no name shows as "a blackout rule".
- If you use webhooks, each moved subscription sends one `subscription_updated` webhook, so a batch of 200 sends up to 200 webhooks. Tell the people who run your integrations before you turn the feature on.
- Customers don't get an email or a text about the move. They see the new date on their subscription.

For example, a rule moves the 29th, 30th and 31st to the 28th, your lead time is 7 days, and today is Oct 25. A renewal on Oct 30 is inside the lead time, so it stays. A renewal on Nov 30 moves to Nov 28.

## What customers see

- **At checkout**: the first renewal date shown for a new subscription is the moved date. If a rule moved it and the rule has a checkout message, the message shows under the renewal date of the subscription line in the order summary. It doesn't show when the subscription joins an autoship profile. The message never stops checkout.
- **When they change a date**: blocked days are greyed out in the date pickers on the customer's subscription page and in the customer portal, bundle date pickers included. If a date they choose lands on a blocked day anyway, for example a pause for a number of months, Fluid saves the first open day after it and shows "Your next order is" with that date, for example "Your next order is 12/1/2026." The message is translated into every language the pages offer. Customers never see a refusal for a blocked day.

## What blackout dates don't change

- The first order is charged the day the customer buys, even on a blocked day.
- Reactivating a subscription with an immediate charge charges today, even on a blocked day.
- When a payment fails, Fluid retries it on its usual schedule, even on a blocked day.
- Blackout dates never stop a renewal that is due. They change which dates are saved.

## The billing day

A monthly or yearly subscription keeps its own billing day, for example the 30th. A rule moves the date, not the billing day. With a rule that moves the 29th through the 31st to the 28th, a subscription with billing day 30 renews on the 28th each month. If the rule goes away, it renews on the 30th again.

A subscription takes the moved day as its billing day when its first renewal date was moved at checkout, or when it resumes or is reactivated onto a moved date. It then stays on that day after the rule goes away.

## Turn blackout dates off

Turn off **Blackout dates** in the **Features** card and click **Save**.

- No date moves and no chosen date is refused for a blocked day from then on.
- Dates already moved stay where they are. At its next renewal, each subscription goes back to its billing day.
- Your rules stay saved. If you turn the switch on again, they apply again.

If Fluid turns off Subscription Pro for your company, the same happens, and your rules and switch are kept.
