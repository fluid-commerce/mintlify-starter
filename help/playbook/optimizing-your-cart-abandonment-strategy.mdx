Many online shoppers add items to their cart and leave without buying. A clear cart abandonment strategy helps you prevent some of those losses and recover others.

## Why shoppers abandon their carts

A shopper fills their cart, reaches checkout, and disappears. Knowing why helps you prevent it.

| Reason | What happens | How to prevent it |
| - | - | - |
| Unexpected costs | Shipping fees surprise them at checkout | Show shipping costs early |
| Forced account creation | They don't want to register to buy | Don't make an account a requirement |
| Price comparison | They leave to compare prices | Make your unique value clear |
| Distractions | A call or an interruption pulls them away | Make it easy to come back to the cart |
| Security worries | They're unsure their payment is safe | Show security badges clearly |

## How Fluid helps you recover carts

When a shopper who entered their email address leaves items in their cart, Fluid can:

- **Prompt the rep to follow up.** If your company turns on the **Cart Abandoned** catchup, the rep who gets credit for the cart sees a follow-up suggestion in the mobile app's **Catch Up**. See [Catchups](/help/admin/settings/catch-ups).
- **Start your recovery emails.** Fluid sends a `cart_abandoned` webhook event, which your email or marketing tool can use to start a recovery sequence. See [Webhooks](/help/admin/settings/webhooks) and the [Checkout](/concepts/checkout) overview.

Fluid doesn't send recovery emails to shoppers itself. Its abandoned-cart notifications go to your reps, not your customers (see [Communications](/help/admin/settings/communications#abandoned-checkout-emails)). Plan your sequence in the tool that sends your marketing email.

## Send a recovery email sequence

Timing matters. A common approach uses three emails.

### A gentle reminder, about an hour later

The first email should feel like a helpful tap on the shoulder:

- Use a friendly, service-minded tone.
- Ask whether they need help completing their purchase.
- Add a simple, prominent button that returns them to their cart.

### A reminder of the value, about 24 hours later

Remind them why they wanted the products. Address common concerns, and highlight what makes your offer different.

### A final opportunity, about 48 hours later

- Present your best offer.
- Give a genuine reason to act now.
- Make the call to action impossible to miss.

## Write emails that convert

Every recovery email should include:

1. Clear product images
2. The original prices
3. A one-click way back to the cart
4. A short path to checkout
5. An easy way to get help

Start with a compelling subject line, lead with empathy, and guide them toward completing their purchase.

## Create real urgency

Nobody likes fake urgency, but honest reasons to act are fine. Tell shoppers when:

- Stock is genuinely limited.
- A special offer really is ending.

Share real customer reviews and ratings where they're relevant.

## Make help easy to find

A shopper with a question may leave instead of asking. Offer more than one way to get help:

- Chat for quick questions
- Email for detailed questions
- Links to answers to common questions
- Clear support hours

## Use incentives wisely

Not every abandoned cart needs a discount. Try a progressive approach:

1. **First email:** a reminder, with no discount.
2. **Second email:** a small incentive, such as free shipping.
3. **Final email:** your best offer.

Incentives other than discounts can work too:

- An express shipping upgrade
- A bonus product with the purchase
- A longer return period
- Extra loyalty points
- Priority customer service

## Measure and test

Watch these numbers:

- Recovery rate over time
- Average order value
- Email opens and clicks
- How long shoppers take to return
- What your incentives cost compared with what they recover

Keep testing:

- Subject lines
- Email timing
- Incentive types
- Message formatting
- Call-to-action placement

<Tip>
  Start simple and let the results guide you. What works for one business might not work for another. Your customers show you what they prefer through what they do.
</Tip>
