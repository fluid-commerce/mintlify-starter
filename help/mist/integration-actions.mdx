Mist can look inside a connected back-office integration and help you fix it. Ask in plain words, such as "Why didn't order 10482 sync to Exigo?" or "What's failing in our Exigo sync?". Mist runs the integration's actions for you: checks that only read, and fixes that change records. A fix never runs until you approve its plan.

Today this works for Exigo. Mist works only on the company you're signed in to.

## Before you start

- Your company has the Exigo connector installed and connected. See [Back-office connectors](/help/admin/droplets/integrations).
- You're signed in to Mist as an admin of the company. Mist acts as you, and it can't use a company API, partner or droplet token for this.
- Your role has the **Integration Ops** permissions you need. On the [Roles](/help/admin/settings/roles) screen, **Integration Ops** is in the **Settings** card. Open it to switch each permission on or off:

| Permission | What it lets you do |
| --- | --- |
| **See and preview integration ops actions and their history** | List the actions, run the checks, preview a fix and see the history. Every other permission needs this one. |
| **Run standard integration ops actions** | Apply fixes marked `run`. Also needed to cancel a running fix. |
| **Run high-risk integration ops actions** | Apply fixes marked `run_guarded`, the riskiest kind. |

Each action has a tier: `view`, `run` or `run_guarded`. You need the permission for an action's tier to preview or apply it. An action that changes records always needs at least **Run standard integration ops actions** to apply, whatever its tier.

## Ask Mist what's failing

Mist works from the safest check to the riskiest change:

1. It lists what's failing, grouped by cause, with how many different records each group affects. See [What failure groups mean](/help/admin/droplets/integrations#what-failure-groups-mean).
2. It checks one record from a group to find out what happened to it.
3. Depending on what it finds, it tests the connection, checks the connector's webhooks, or checks your mappings.
4. Only when the cause is fixed does it offer to retry the records that failed.

Checks that only read are their own answer. Mist shows you the result and every warning, and there's nothing to approve.

What Mist can check, all with the `view` tier:

- What's failing, grouped by cause. Groups whose source data needs fixing are counted separately, because a retry won't help them.
- What happened to one record.
- Whether Fluid can reach and sign in to Exigo. A passing test doesn't guarantee that every sync works.
- Whether the connector's webhook subscriptions are all there and active.
- Whether your mappings have gaps, blank matches, or values that differ only by case or spaces.
- The products reconciliation. Mist reads the last saved check and never runs a new one. If there's no recent check, open **Reconciliation** on the connector's screen and click **Check now**, then ask again. See [Check reconciliation](/help/admin/droplets/integrations#check-reconciliation).

Fixes Mist can plan include retrying failed records (`run`) and registering missing webhooks or turning inactive ones back on (`run_guarded`). Mist reads the full list of actions from your integration, with each action's tier, before it uses one. A retry follows the same rules as a replay on the connector's screen. See [Which records you can replay](/help/admin/droplets/integrations#which-records-you-can-replay).

## Approve a fix

When a fix would change records, Mist first previews it. The preview changes nothing in your integration, but it's recorded in the history.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read the plan">
    Mist tells you what the fix will do: how many records, which ones, and any warnings, such as changes that can't be undone. Read every warning.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approve or decline">
    An approval card appears in the chat. Click **Approve** to run exactly that plan, or **Decline** to stop. The card then reads **Approved** or **Declined**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the result">
    Mist runs the fix and follows it until it finishes. A fix ends as succeeded, partly succeeded, failed or cancelled. Mist tells you which.
  </Step>
</Steps>

An approval covers one plan, once, for 15 minutes. If the plan changes, or the connector's settings change before the fix runs, Mist previews again and asks you again.

To stop a fix that's still waiting or running, ask Mist to cancel it. A fix that's waiting stops at once. A running fix stops as soon as it can. A finished fix can't be cancelled.

If an action has an undo action, Mist tells you its name. Mist doesn't run it for you.

## When Mist stops

Mist doesn't retry on its own. It stops and tells you what it checked when:

| What Mist tells you | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Writes are turned off (`writes_disabled`) | Fluid has fixes that change records turned off. Previews still work, so you can see the plan. | Don't look for another way to make the change. If you need the fix, contact Fluid support. |
| You don't have permission | Your role lacks the permission for the action's tier. | Ask an admin who manages roles to update your role on the [Roles](/help/admin/settings/roles) screen. |
| The plan can't be applied any more | The preview expired, was already used, or the request or the connector's settings changed since the preview. | Let Mist preview again and approve the new plan. If the plan was already used, Mist checks the history first, so the fix doesn't run twice. |
| No confirmed answer when applying | The fix may have run anyway. | Let Mist check the fix's status and history before anything else. Don't approve it again. |
| The integration service is unavailable | Fluid couldn't reach the integration. | Try again later. |

Mist also stops when a retry fails again for the same reason, when the cause is on your back office's side, such as credentials or data only you can correct, or when a plan affects far more records than you expected.
