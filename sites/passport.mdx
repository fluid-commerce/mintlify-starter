A Site opens inside your storefront's page. The storefront keeps the parts that need its own sign-in, cart and credit, and draws them around your Site. This page covers those parts, what each one gives your Site, and what you can change.

## What the storefront adds around your Site

| Part | What it is | Who sees it |
| - | - | - |
| **The Passport** | The storefront's account menu: a round button in a corner that opens the member's account, orders, subscriptions, their sites menu and sign-out | A signed-in member |
| **Lead capture** | FairShare's button that asks a guest for their details for the member who shared the link | A guest |
| **The cart button** | FairShare's floating cart, when your company shows one | Everyone |
| **Cart drawer and checkout** | The storefront's own cart and checkout, which `fluid.cart` opens | Everyone |

A signed-in member sees the Passport and never the lead-capture button. A guest sees the lead-capture button and never the Passport. When the visitor signs in or out, the two swap in place.

## The Passport

The Passport is the storefront's account menu. On a Site it does three jobs for you:

- **It's the visitor's account.** Orders, subscriptions, addresses, payment methods and account settings open from it, so your Site doesn't build any of them.
- **It lists the member's Sites.** Its sites menu lists the Sites the member can open, including any your company pinned for them. See [Show a Site to members](/sites/overview#show-a-site-to-members).
- **It holds the sign-out.** A Site can't sign anyone out. The visitor signs out in the Passport, and confirms it there.

## Sign-in carries over from the storefront

A member or customer who is signed in on your storefront is already signed in on every Site there. There's no second login and no sign-in screen of your own to build.

```ts
import { fluid } from "@fluid-app/sites";

const me = await fluid.auth.me();
if (me.viewer) {
  greeting.textContent = `Welcome back, ${me.viewer.name}`;
}
```

`fluid.auth.me()` gives your Site the visitor's name, avatar, user and member ids, their relation to the Site (`owner`, `admin` or `member`), and only the member fields your Site declares in `config/access.json`. See [Read who is here](/sites/sdk#read-who-is-here) and [Fluid data the site uses](/sites/declarations#fluid-data-the-site-uses).

### How it stays secure

Your Site's code runs on its own host under `sites.fluid.app`, in a sandboxed frame on the storefront's page. Because that host is a different origin from your storefront:

- **Your Site never receives the visitor's Fluid credentials.** It can't read the storefront's sign-in cookie or any Fluid token, and neither can a script you add to your Site.
- **The storefront hands your Site a short-lived session.** It's scoped to that one Site and that visitor, and the SDK renews it while the page is open. The SDK sends it only to your Site's own host.
- **Every call acts as the visitor.** `fluid.api` and `ctx.fluid.api` reach only that visitor's own data, within the scopes your Site declares. A guest's call goes as a guest.
- **A private Site's files stay private.** Its pages, scripts and functions aren't served to someone the Site doesn't admit, even when they open its host directly.
- **Sign-in takes a click.** `fluid.auth.signIn()` sends the visitor to the storefront's own sign-in only from a click, so a Site can't redirect visitors to it on load.

## Hide the floating cart and lead-capture buttons

To hide FairShare's floating cart and lead-capture buttons, such as when your Site draws its own controls in that corner, add `config/shell.json` to your Site and publish it:

```json config/shell.json
{ "fairshare": "hidden" }
```

Only the floating buttons go. `fluid.cart`, the cart drawer, checkout and the visit's credit keep working, so give visitors your own cart button with `fluid.cart.open()` and guests your own sign-in with `fluid.auth.signIn()`. Publish the file with Sites plugin 0.1.379 or later. See [The storefront around the Site](/sites/declarations#the-storefront-around-the-site).

<Note>
`"fairshare": "hidden"` doesn't hide the Passport. A signed-in member always sees the Passport's button, because it holds their sign-out. A guest never sees it.
</Note>

## Open the Passport from your own button

To open the Passport from a button of your own, such as an avatar in your Site's header, call `fluid.auth.signOut()` from the click:

```tsx
import { fluid } from "@fluid-app/sites";

function AccountButton({ signedIn }: { signedIn: boolean }) {
  return signedIn ? (
    <button type="button" onClick={() => fluid.auth.signOut()}>
      My account
    </button>
  ) : (
    <button type="button" onClick={() => fluid.auth.signIn()}>
      Sign in
    </button>
  );
}
```

- **`signOut()` signs nobody out.** It opens the Passport, where the visitor can sign out and confirm it.
- **It needs a signed-in visitor.** A guest has no Passport, so the call does nothing for a guest. Send a guest to `fluid.auth.signIn()` instead, as above.
- **It needs the storefront.** Outside a storefront, such as on the Site's own host, it does nothing and logs a warning.

## What the storefront loads, and what it costs your Site

The storefront page around your Site loads the Passport, FairShare (the cart, lead capture and the visit's credit) and a small script that connects your Site to them. Each one loads as a deferred module script, so none of them blocks the page.

- **Your Site paints on its own.** It loads in its own frame, in parallel with the storefront's scripts. If the storefront's scripts are slow or fail, your Site still opens and works. Only the parts they draw, such as the Passport and the cart, are missing.
- **The SDK is yours to load.** The storefront doesn't add anything to your Site's own pages. Import `@fluid-app/sites` in your build, or load `/_fluid/sdk/v1/sites.js` from your Site's own host as the [plain HTML guide](/sites/frameworks/html) shows. Load it as a module, so it never blocks your first paint.
- **A call waits for the sign-in, briefly.** A call made before the SDK has connected to the storefront waits for it, for up to three seconds, then goes as a guest.
