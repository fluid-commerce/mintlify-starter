A Site is a static web app that Fluid hosts for your company: a landing page, an event page, a calculator, a sign-up page or a small dashboard. You build it like any static app, Vite and React by default, and publish its build with the Fluid CLI, Mist or an AI agent. It opens on your storefront, inside the storefront's own cart, sign-in and account menu.

There's one kind of Site: a bundle of built files, plus the declarations in `db/` and `config/`. Sites used to be called member sites. A Site isn't a theme's member site, which is written in Liquid and is deprecated, and it isn't MySite.

<Note>
  Fluid turns Sites on for your company. Until they're on, publishing answers `404`. To ask for them, [contact support](/help/getting-help).
</Note>

## Where a Site opens

A published Site opens at its storefront address:

```text
https://yoli.com/home/sites/diamond-leaders
https://yoli.com/sites/diamond-leaders/events
```

`diamond-leaders` is the Site's handle: lowercase letters, digits and single hyphens, at most 40 characters, unique in your company. Everything after the handle is the Site's own path. The storefront API calls the handle the Site's `slug`.

| Address | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `/home/sites/<handle>` | The canonical address. Search engines and link previews name this one |
| `/sites/<handle>` | The same Site. It's the address the API returns as a Site's `url` |
| `/<username>/sites/<handle>` | A member's share link. It credits that member for what the visitor buys |
| `<label>.sites.fluid.app` | The Site's own host, where its code runs. See [Standalone hosting](/sites/standalone-hosting) |

A visit through `/home/sites/<handle>` or `/sites/<handle>` credits the Site's owner, or the company for a company site.

Sites used to open under `/accounts/`. Those addresses no longer open a Site, and they don't redirect, so update any link that still uses one. Account screens such as `/accounts/profile` aren't Sites and keep their addresses.

The storefront draws the page around your Site:

- **The storefront's own parts.** The cart drawer, checkout, sign-in and the account menu belong to the storefront. Your Site uses them through the SDK, so member pricing and the visit's credit are the storefront's own. A signed-in member sees the account menu's button in the corner. A guest sees the storefront's lead-capture widget there instead, with no sign-in button, so give guests your own with `fluid.auth.signIn()`.
- **One frame for your Site.** Your Site runs in one full-page frame, served from its own host under `sites.fluid.app`. The frame is sandboxed, so your code can't move the storefront's window by script. Link to storefront pages with plain `<a href>` links, and the SDK opens them in the storefront's window when the visitor clicks. See [Link to the storefront](/sites/sdk#link-to-the-storefront).
- **One address bar.** The storefront keeps the address bar and the history. Back and Forward move through your Site's pages, one entry per page.
- **Page titles and previews.** The storefront writes each page's head from the Site's SEO settings and `config/meta.json`. See [SEO and indexing](/sites/seo).

If someone opens your Site's own host directly, they land on its storefront address, so your Site opens inside the storefront. The exception is a public company Site with [standalone hosting](/sites/standalone-hosting), which also serves on its own host.

## Who can open a Site

A Site belongs to a member or to the company. A new Site is private until its owner makes it public.

| Visibility | Who can open it |
| --- | --- |
| `public` | Anyone, signed in or not |
| `private` | The owner, company admins, and members a share admits |

A Site that's switched off (`active: false`) opens only for its owner and company admins, whatever its visibility. A [branch](/sites/source-and-branches#branches) is private and opens only for the owner and company admins. See [Switch a site off](/storefront/sites#switch-a-site-off).

Sites are shared within one company. A visitor who isn't a member of your company is a guest, and a guest opens public Sites only.

A visitor a Site doesn't admit sees a page in your company's branding:

- A guest sees **Sign in to see this page**, with **Sign in** and **Return to storefront**. After signing in, they come back to the same page.
- A signed-in member sees **You need access**, with the account they're signed in as, so they can sign out and switch. On a member's private Site, they can also [ask the owner for access](#ask-for-access).

The page looks the same for a Site that doesn't exist, a draft, a Site that was taken down and a private Site, so it never tells a visitor that a Site exists.

If a Site's page can't load, the storefront tries once more. If that fails too, the visitor sees **This page didn't load**, with **Try again** and **Return to storefront**.

## Share a Site

A member's own share link, the Site's `share_url` when the member reads the Site through the Member API, carries their credit, such as `https://acme.fluid.app/janedoe/sites/diamond-leaders`, so the member who shared it gets credit for what the visitor buys.

**Copy link** copies the Site's plain link. You find it in Mist and in the site's screen in the Fluid admin. It shows once the Site is published.

### Who manages what

| Task | Who does it | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Make a Site public or private | The owner. For a company Site, a company admin | `fluid sites visibility public` or `private`, with the owner's login. A company admin can also change a company Site's visibility in the Fluid admin or in Mist |
| Choose who opens a private Site | The owner shares a member's Site with a member, their downline, or a segment by member type and country. A company admin shares a company Site with a member or a segment | **Share** in Mist's preview pane, for a company Site. Or [Share a site](/api-reference/site-shares/share-a-site) and [Share a company site](/api-reference/member-site-shares/share-a-company-site) |
| Invite a member to view or edit a member's Site | The owner | The Member API: [Invite a member to a site](/api-reference/site-invitations/invite-a-member-to-a-site). See [Invite a member to edit](#invite-a-member-to-edit) |
| Answer a request for access | The owner of a member's Site | **Review request** in the email Fluid sends, or [Approve an access request](/api-reference/site-access-requests/approve-an-access-request) |
| See and remove a Site's shares | A company admin | The Site's **Shared with** card in the Fluid admin |
| Take a Site down | A company admin | **Take down** on the Site's screen in the Fluid admin, or the API. Both take an optional reason. Its owner can't publish, schedule, switch on or make public the Site until an admin brings it back, but can still edit it. See [When a site is taken down](/storefront/sites#when-a-site-is-taken-down) |
| Bring a Site back | A company admin | **Bring back** on the Site's screen in the Fluid admin |

In the Fluid admin, Sites are under **Website** > **Sites**. See [Sites in the admin](/help/admin/member-sites). The admin doesn't add shares. Share a company Site from Mist, or through the API.

### Open a company Site to every member

To let every signed-in member of your company open a company Site, while guests are still asked to sign in:

1. Keep the Site private, and publish it.
2. [Share the company site](/api-reference/member-site-shares/share-a-company-site) with a segment that names no member type and no country.

That one share admits every member, including members who join later, and the Site appears in their account menu. To admit fewer members, name a member type, a country, or both. To stop members from removing the Site from their menu, turn on **Required** in the Site's **Settings** card in the Fluid admin, then click **Save**. To let guests open it too, make it public instead.

### Tell members about a share

When a Site is shared with a member, Fluid sends them a direct message in Flow with the Site's link. This covers a share with one member, a downline or a segment, of a member's Site or a company Site. Each member a new share reaches gets one message. A member another share already reached, the owner and the member who shared it get none. An invitation sends a message too.

### Invite a member to edit

The owner of a member's Site can invite another member of the company to view it or to edit it. Invitations work through the Member API only. Mist and the account menu don't send or accept them yet.

1. The owner [invites the member](/api-reference/site-invitations/invite-a-member-to-a-site) with the role `view` or `edit`. The member gets a Flow message.
2. The member [lists their invitations](/api-reference/site-invitations/list-the-members-site-invitations) and [accepts](/api-reference/site-invitations/accept-a-site-invitation) or [declines](/api-reference/site-invitations/decline-a-site-invitation) one. Declining doesn't tell the owner, and the member can [undo it](/api-reference/site-invitations/undo-declining-a-site-invitation).

An invitation lasts 7 days. Inviting the same member again sends it again with the new role and a fresh 7 days. The owner can [list](/api-reference/site-invitations/list-a-sites-invitations) and [revoke](/api-reference/site-invitations/revoke-a-site-invitation) a Site's invitations. A company Site takes no invitations; a company admin shares it instead.

A member who accepts `edit` becomes an editor. An editor can read the Site's files, change its drafts and its branches' drafts, and open the draft preview. An editor can't publish the Site, share it, copy it, delete it, change its visibility or settings, or create or delete a branch. An editor also can't [pull its source](/sites/source-and-branches), so building and publishing stays with the owner.

### Ask for access

A signed-in member a member's private Site doesn't admit can ask its owner for access. On **You need access**, they add a message if they like, up to 500 characters, and click **Request access**. The page then says **Request sent**.

The owner gets an email with the member's name and message and a **Review request** button. It opens a page on your storefront where the owner clicks **Approve** or **Decline**. The owner can also [list](/api-reference/site-access-requests/list-a-sites-access-requests), [approve](/api-reference/site-access-requests/approve-an-access-request) and [decline](/api-reference/site-access-requests/decline-an-access-request) requests through the Member API.

- **Approve** shares the Site with the member to view. Fluid emails them, and sends the Flow message for a new share.
- **Decline** tells no one. The member can ask again.

A request reaches the owner only for a published, private Site a member owns, when your company's messaging rules let the owner share with the member. A company Site never takes requests. A member has one open request per Site, the owner gets at most one from them a day, and a request expires after 30 days.

## Company Sites on the storefront

A Site your company owns is also a storefront resource, like a page or a product. It has SEO, metafields, countries, a `draft`, `scheduled`, `published` or `archived` status and an on/off switch, and the public storefront API lists it once it's live. Member-owned Sites are never in that list. See [Company sites on the storefront](/storefront/sites).

## Start from the template

`npm create @fluid-app/fluid-site` and `fluid sites init` start a Site in an empty folder. The default starter is a copy of the public [member-site-template](https://github.com/Fluid-WeCommerce/member-site-template) repository, a blank Vite and React app. It builds and publishes as it is, and it has:

- one home page, and a not-found page for any other path;
- a small router that follows the storefront's Back and Forward;
- CSS tokens at the top of `src/styles.css`, the only place a color or size is named;
- `fluidSite()` in `vite.config.ts`, which writes the SEO files at build time;
- an empty `config/meta.json`, so the Site's SEO settings decide its title and description;
- `config/access.json` with no scopes.

It has no shop and no `db/` folder, so a new Site has no database until you add a table. See [The Site database](/sites/database).

```text
diamond-leaders/
  .gitignore
  README.md
  index.html
  package.json
  tsconfig.json
  vite.config.ts        adds fluidSite() from @fluid-app/sites/vite
  src/
    main.tsx            imports the SDK once
    App.tsx             the pages, by path
    Link.tsx            a link to another page of the Site
    router.ts           a small router that follows Back and Forward
    styles.css          the CSS tokens
    pages/
      Home.tsx          the home page
      NotFound.tsx      the page for any other path
  config/
    access.json         the Fluid API scopes the Site asks for: none
    meta.json           {}: SEO comes from the Site's settings
```

To add a page, add a component in `src/pages/`, add it to the pages in `src/App.tsx`, and link to it with `<Link>`.

`db/schema.json` and the files in `config/` are your Site's declarations. They're published with the build and never served as files. Each is optional: leave one out and the Site gets its default.

| File | What it declares | Page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `db/schema.json` | The Site's tables, their columns and who may read and write them | [Database](/sites/database) |
| `config/access.json` | The Fluid API scopes and member fields the Site uses | [Declarations](/sites/declarations#fluid-data-the-site-uses) |
| `config/meta.json` | Page titles, previews, routes and the Site's address for the sitemap | [SEO and indexing](/sites/seo#the-page-metadata-file) |
| `config/routes.json` | Which file answers each path | [Declarations](/sites/declarations#routes) |

## Build in Mist

When Sites are on for your company, Mist has a **Sites** section where you create a company Site from the template and open it. You can also ask Mist in chat to create a Site, or to grow one: add pages, a shop with add to cart and checkout, storefront links that keep the visit's credit, a database table, or content for signed-in members.

- **Run** starts the Site in the preview with `fluid sites dev`, on a private local database. Its rows stay on your computer.
- The **Local** and **Prod** previews run as the member you're signed in to Mist as, so `fluid.auth.me()` and `fluid.api` answer for you. The cart still needs the storefront page around the Site, so check it on the published Site.
- **Publish** builds the Site and publishes it. When a change to `db/schema.json` would delete data, Mist lists each step in plain words and asks before it applies it.
- **Share**, on a company Site, opens its sharing settings:
  - **Invite people**: find a member by name or email and give them **Can view** or **Can copy**. It shares the Site at once, and the member gets a Flow message.
  - **People with access**: change or remove each share.
  - **General access**: switch between **Public** and **Private**. While the Site is private, **Add people who match** shares it with everyone of a member type, a country, or both.
  - **Admins only**: turn on **Pin this site for everyone** to list it first for everyone it's shared with, without letting them remove it.
  - **Visit** opens the Site and **Copy link** copies its link. Both work once the Site is published.
- The settings rail sets the Site's name, visibility, status and search engine settings. See [SEO and indexing](/sites/seo).

## What Sites don't do

- **No custom domain.** A Site lives at your company's addresses. A project that needs its own domain is a [Mist app](/sites/site-or-app).
- **Only light server code.** A Site's [server functions](/sites/functions) run up to 30 seconds and 1 second of CPU a call, on Cloudflare Workers without Node.js built-in modules. Heavier server work belongs in a [Mist app](/sites/site-or-app).
- **No service workers.** The Site's host refuses a service worker's script.
- **No files under `/_fluid/`.** The Site's host answers that path itself, and publishing refuses a build with a file under it.
- **No SQL from the browser.** Your code reads and writes its tables through `fluid.db`, and the Site's table rules decide each request.
- **No secrets in the Site.** Everything in your build is public to whoever can open the Site. Don't put an API key or a token in it.
- **No visitors from other companies.** A person who isn't a member of your company opens your Sites as a guest.

When a Site outgrows these limits, [convert it to an app](/sites/convert-to-app).
