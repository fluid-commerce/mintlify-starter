Two things decide who can open a member site: its visibility and its shares. Visibility is a setting on the site. Shares are a list of audiences you add to it. This page covers both, who can open a site in each case, and what a share grants and doesn't.

## Choose a visibility

A site is `private` or `public`. A new site is private. You set it when you [add the site](/api-reference/sites/add-a-site) or [change it](/api-reference/sites/change-a-site). A company admin sets a company site's visibility with [Change a member site](/api-reference/member-sites/change-a-member-site). The change takes effect as soon as you save it.

Visibility applies only once a site is published. A draft site or a taken-down site isn't open to members whatever its visibility says. See [Know draft and published](/member-sites/publishing#know-draft-and-published).

A published `public` site is also listed on its owner's public sites page, at `/<credit>/sites`. A private site, a draft and a site that is only shared never are. See [A member's public sites page](/member-sites/public-sites).

## Know who can open a site

The answer depends on who is asking and on the site's state.

| Viewer | A published public site | A published private site |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A guest | Opens | Sent to sign in |
| The site's owner | Opens | Opens |
| Another signed-in member | Opens | Opens only if a share matches them. Otherwise not found |
| A company admin | Opens | Opens |

- **A guest is never told whether a site exists.** A guest who asks for anything but a published public site is sent to sign in, and a missing site and a private site look the same.
- **A signed-in member outside the audience gets not found.** The answer is the same as for a site that doesn't exist.
- **A draft and a taken-down site open for no member.** The owner and company admins included. Preview a draft instead. See [Preview the draft](/member-sites/publishing#preview-the-draft).
- **A locked site opens only for company admins.** Its owner is told the company locked it, and everyone else gets not found. See [Lock a site](/member-sites/publishing#lock-a-site).
- **A company site's owner is every company admin.** A member's site has one owner, the member who built it. An admin who isn't the owner opens a member's published private site as an admin.
- **A company admin is a signed-in member whose user is an admin of the company.** Admin access to a site's files and pages is separate: see [Build with the APIs](/member-sites/apis).
- **A site whose owner is no longer an active member opens only for company admins.** That holds whether it is public or private.
- **A branch is private and unshareable.** Only its owner and company admins open it.

A site opens at `/accounts/<site-handle>` on your company's own domain. See [what a member site is](/member-sites/overview#what-a-member-site-is).

## Serve a company site to your members

A company site is built by your company's admins for your members. Admins create and edit it; members are its audience. It isn't an admins-only site.

- **Every member:** make it `public`. Guests open it too.
- **Some members, by role or country:** keep it `private` and share it with a segment: a member type, a country, or both. A member a segment matches opens it; any other member gets not found. Add one share per segment you want to serve, because shares combine. This replaces the countries and member types of theme member sites, which are deprecated. See [Share a site](#share-a-site).
- **Named members:** share it with a member, the same way. A downline share isn't available for a company site, because a company admin can't make one.
- **Members can't hide it:** mark it `required` with [Change a member site](/api-reference/member-sites/change-a-member-site). A member it reaches can't remove a required company site from their account menu. See [Sites home](/member-sites/sites-home).

A share a company admin makes isn't limited by the company's member messaging policy. See [Follow the messaging policy](#follow-the-messaging-policy).

## Share a site

A share names one audience and a permission. Add as many as you need. They combine, and a member who matches several shares gets the highest permission.

| Audience | Who it reaches |
| --- | --- |
| One member | The member you name |
| The sharer's downline | Every member below the member who made the share, on the enrollment tree. A company admin can't make this share, because a downline belongs to a member |
| A segment | The company's members of a member type, in a country, both, or neither. A segment with neither reaches every member the sharer can reach |

A member's country is their user's country, else the company's default country. A segment matches a blank field to any value.

| Permission | What the audience can do |
| --- | --- |
| `view` | Open the site |
| `copy` | Open the site, and copy it into a site of their own |

For a member's site, the member who owns it shares it. For a company site, a company admin shares it. Sharing a member's site is the member's to do, so an admin can't share it.

### Use the shares API

- **A member's shares:** [List a site's shares](/api-reference/site-shares/list-a-sites-shares), [Share a site](/api-reference/site-shares/share-a-site), [Change a share's permission](/api-reference/site-shares/change-a-shares-permission) and [Stop sharing with an audience](/api-reference/site-shares/stop-sharing-with-an-audience).
- **A company site's shares, and any site's shares for an admin:** [List a site's shares](/api-reference/member-site-shares/list-a-sites-shares), [Share a company site](/api-reference/member-site-shares/share-a-company-site), [Change a share's permission](/api-reference/member-site-shares/change-a-shares-permission) and [Delete a share](/api-reference/member-site-shares/delete-a-share).
- **Find whom a member can share with:** [Search the share targets](/api-reference/site-share-targets/search-the-share-targets) lists matching members, the member types and countries a segment can name, and which audiences the member's company allows.

A few rules to know:

- **Sharing the same audience twice writes nothing.** The request returns the share that exists, with its permission unchanged. Change the permission with [Change a share's permission](/api-reference/site-shares/change-a-shares-permission).
- **A share changes only its permission.** To change who it names, delete it and add another.
- **A share never names an email address.** It names a member, a member type or a country.
- **You can't share with yourself or with a member of another company.** Both are refused, and so is a member type or country the company doesn't have.
- **An admin can list and delete the shares of any site in the company.** An admin can't create or change the shares of a member's site.
- **A deleted share closes access on the member's next request.** Another share that still matches keeps it open.

## Follow the messaging policy

A member shares the way a member messages. The company's [Member messaging policy](/api/guides/messaging#set-what-members-can-see) limits whom a member can share with, and a share a member made is checked against it each time someone opens the site, not only when the share is made.

| `directory_scope` | What a member can share with |
| --- | --- |
| `all_members` | Any audience. A member share can name any active member of the company |
| `team_graph` | One member in the sharer's Team Graph, or the sharer's downline. A segment is refused |
| `no_direct_messages` | No one |

- **A company without a saved policy behaves as `all_members`.**
- **A share the policy no longer allows stops working.** Narrowing the policy closes the access of those shares with nobody rewriting them, and widening it brings them back.
- **A share a company admin made isn't limited by the policy.** It matches under every setting.
- **A member who isn't in the sharer's Team Graph gets nothing from that share.** A member or sharer with no place in the graph is in no Team Graph.

[Search the share targets](/api-reference/site-share-targets/search-the-share-targets) tells a member which audiences their policy allows. It allows 30 requests a minute, counted together with the messaging recipient search.

## Know what a share grants

A share is a way in, and it lets the site's script use the person's own data. It grants:

- **Opening a private site.** A member the share matches opens a published private site.
- **Copying, with `copy` permission.** A member can copy a site they can open when a share with `copy` matches them. A member who can open a site but has no `copy` share is refused. See [Copy a site](/member-sites/publishing#copy-a-site).
- **A place in the member's menu.** A published site a share reaches a member for appears in that member's account menu. See [Sites home](/member-sites/sites-home).
- **The person's own data, in the site's script.** The script calls the member API as the person who opened the site. It reads and changes that person's data, within what the site's published declaration lists and outside the scopes in [Know what a share never grants](#know-what-a-share-never-grants). The person isn't asked first. There is no prompt and no consent step.

A share doesn't grant:

- **Changes.** A share holder can't edit, publish, delete or share the site. Only the owner can, and an admin can take it down.
- **The owner's data.** The script never reads the owner's data and never gets the owner's session. It acts as the person who opened the site, and as no one else.
- **Anything the site didn't declare.** A site with no declaration, or one that lists nothing, gives a share holder nothing. The share holder opens the site, and its script gets no data. See [Declare what a member site uses](/member-sites/access-declarations).
- **Featuring or pinning.** A company that features a site shares it with no one, and a member who pins a site gets no access from the pin.
- **Access to a draft.** A share on a draft or taken-down site opens nothing until the site is published.
- **Anything beyond the policy.** A share reaches only the members the sharer's messaging policy lets it reach.

A member can remove a shared site from their own menu. That changes what their menu lists. It never changes who can open the site by its address.

### Know who gets data through a share

A share holder is a signed-in member whom one of the site's shares matches now: a member the share names, the sharer's downline, a segment, or the audience of a company site's shares. A published public site has share holders too, and so does an admin who holds a share.

- **A viewer no share matches gets no data.** That holds for a guest, for another member and for an admin who holds no share, on a public site too.
- **A company site's audience gets data too.** A company site has no owner, so only its share holders get data, and only when an admin published a declaration for it. A company site with no declaration gives no one data.
- **A change closes the next call.** A deleted share, a narrower messaging policy, a take-down, an owner who is no longer active and a publish that drops a scope each refuse the next call. A declaration you only drafted changes nothing.

See [Know who gets data](/member-sites/site-scripts#know-who-gets-data) for what each viewer reaches.

### Know what a share never grants

A share holder is refused these scopes whether or not the site declares them, because each one delivers outside Fluid, reaches other people, spends the company's credits, records the person's consent, or reads other people's personal data.

| Scope | Why a share holder never gets it |
| --- | --- |
| `messages: write` | Sends messages to other members |
| `shares: write` | Makes shares and referral links to send to others |
| `widgets: write` | Schedules a share to other members from a native widget |
| `contacts: read` and `contacts: write` | A rep's contacts hold other people's names, emails, phones and addresses, so a share holder can't read them. Changing a contact is reported to the company's contact webhook |
| `carts: write` | Creates and claims carts, which run the company's cart callbacks with the person's email |
| `mysite: write` | Changes MySite settings, such as the username, which Fluid reports to the company's user webhook, and the signature on the person's outgoing messages |
| `agreements: write` | Records the person's acceptance of an agreement |
| `ai: write` | Sends text to the AI service and spends the company's AI credits |
| `database: read` and `database: write` | The site's own database belongs to its owner |

Three routes are refused inside a family a share holder otherwise gets:

- **`PATCH /api/member/v2026-10/me`**, in `profile: write`. It changes the member's details and triggers the company's member webhooks.
- **`PATCH /api/member/v2026-10/notifications/preferences`**, in `notifications: write`. It turns the person's push, email and SMS notifications on or off, an opt-in.
- **`POST /api/member/v2026-10/calendar/events/:id/rsvp`**, in `calendar: write`. It records the person's answer to an invitation.

A refused call gets a `403` with the body `member_site_unavailable`, and the script can't tell it from a site that doesn't exist. Fluid's page itself refuses the native widget routes that message or share, with `bridge_refused`. A script can still read the other families above, such as `messages: read`, when the site declares the read. `contacts` and `database` are the families whose reads are refused too.

The reads that go through are the person's own. A share holder's `orders: read` lists only the orders the person bought. An order credited to them as a rep is left out of the list, and showing it answers `404`. The owner of the site still sees both.

The rest of a declared family goes through as the person's own change: `tasks`, `media`, personal calendar entries, marking notifications read, and `profile: write` for the person's profile and social links.

<Warning>
  A share holder's script can change the person's display name, bio, photo and social links, and the change shows to company admins and other members in every company the person belongs to. The person takes no action. Declare `profile: write` only when the site needs it.

  A site's script runs with the share holder's own data for every scope the site declares, and the script can send what it read to an address its author chose by navigating the page away. Sharing a site that declares read scopes means trusting its author with each viewer's data. See [Know where a site loads files from](/member-sites/site-scripts#know-where-a-site-loads-files-from).
</Warning>
