Member sites v2 replaces theme member sites, which are documented at [Member sites](/themes/member-storefront/member-sites), are deprecated and will be removed. Build new sites with member sites v2. It lets members and your company build their own pages. Theme member sites are access rules over pages in a storefront theme. A member site in v2 has its own layouts, pages, sections, snippets, assets and menus, and publishing or switching a theme never touches it.

<Note>
  Member sites must be turned on for your company by Fluid. Until they are, every member site API request returns `404`. A site script's calls to the member API also need the member API to be served on your company's storefront, which Fluid turns on too; without it, those calls return `404` even when member sites are on. To ask for either, [contact support](/help/getting-help).
</Note>

## What a member site is

A member site is a small set of pages that a company or one of its members builds. Members open it on your company's own domain:

```text
https://shop.example.com/accounts/rileys-shop/about
```

The path is `/accounts/<site-handle>/<page-slug>`. A site's home page is `/accounts/<site-handle>`.

A site's pages don't render in the page you open. Fluid draws its own page around them and loads the site into a sandboxed frame on the same domain. The browser treats the frame as an anonymous origin, so a site's code can't use the signed-in member's login on its own. A site's JavaScript asks Fluid for the member's data instead, with `fluid.fetch`. See [Read viewer data in site scripts](/member-sites/site-scripts).

Fluid also draws the account menu outside the frame on every page. A site can't hide or replace it.

## Know who owns a site

Every site has one owner kind.

| Owner | Who builds it | What company admins can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A member | The member, for themselves. A member owns at most 25 sites | List, read, take down, delete and copy it. An admin can't change a member's site |
| The company | A company admin | Everything, including building and publishing it |

A member's credentials build a member's sites, and a company admin's build the company's. The [CLI page](/member-sites/cli#choose-the-login) shows which login to use.

## Know a site's status

A site is in one of three states.

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| `draft` | The site is new. Members can't open it, its owner included. Preview it while you build |
| `published` | Members who are allowed to open it see its published pages |
| `taken_down` | A company admin took it down. Members can't open it, and its owner can't publish it again |

## Publish a site

Everything you write is a draft until you publish the site. Members see only published copies of pages, layouts, sections, snippets and settings. A file that was never published renders as missing.

A few changes have no draft and take effect on a published site as soon as you write them: assets, `config/menus.json` and page rows. [Pages and menus](/member-sites/pages-and-menus#know-what-is-live-at-once) lists each one.

Each publish is saved as a version of the site. You can list a site's versions and restore one with the API. A company can take a site down, and you can copy a site, try a change on a branch or delete a site. [Publish, restore, copy and take down a member site](/member-sites/publishing) covers each, and [List a site's versions](/api-reference/sites/list-a-sites-versions) and [Restore a site to a version](/api-reference/sites/restore-a-site-to-a-version) are the operations.

To publish with the CLI, run `fluid member-site publish`. To publish with the API, see [Publish a site](/api-reference/sites/publish-a-site) for a member's site and [Publish a company site](/api-reference/member-sites/publish-a-company-site).

## Choose who can open a site

A published site is `private` or `public`. A new site is private.

| Visibility | Who can open it |
| --- | --- |
| `public` | Anyone, signed in or not |
| `private` | The owner, company admins, and members a share matches. A signed-in member outside that audience gets a not-found answer, so the response never says whether the site exists |

Sharing opens a private site to the members you choose. A share names one audience and a permission:

- **Audience.** One member, the sharing member's downline, or a segment of the company's members. A segment is a member type, a country, both, or neither, which means every member the sharer can reach.
- **Permission.** `view` opens the site. `copy` also lets the member copy it into a site of their own.

A company's messaging policy limits who a member can share with. Sharing never lets anyone edit a site, and it never gives a share holder the owner's data. A share holder's script reads the share holder's own data, within what the site declared. Only the owner of a member's site can share it. A company admin shares the company's sites.

To copy a site, a member needs `copy` permission or must own it. A copy holds the original's published layouts, sections, snippets, pages, settings, text assets and menus, and starts as a draft that belongs to the member. See [Copy a site](/api-reference/sites/copy-a-site).

A published public site is also listed on its owner's public sites page, at `/<credit>/sites`, where `<credit>` is the member's username. [A member's public sites page](/member-sites/public-sites) covers what the page lists, what it leaves out and the storefront API that returns the same list.

[Share a member site](/member-sites/sharing) covers who can open a site in each case, what a share grants and how the messaging policy limits it. For the operations, see [Share a site](/api-reference/site-shares/share-a-site) for members and [Share a company site](/api-reference/member-site-shares/share-a-company-site) for admins.

## Find sites in the account menu

A member's account menu lists the sites they can open in four groups: the company's sites, the sites they built, the sites shared with them, and the sites they removed. Read it with [Show the member's account menu](/api-reference/site-menu/show-the-members-account-menu).

Members can arrange their own menu. None of these changes affects anyone else, and none gives access to a site the member can't already open.

- **Remove a site from the menu.** The site moves to the removed group. A member can't remove their own sites or a company site the company requires.
- **Pin sites.** Pinned sites come first, in an order the member chooses. A member can pin up to 50 sites.
- **Choose a home site.** The member's account opens on their home site while they can still open it. The site must be published.

Company admins can feature up to 20 sites. A featured site is listed first, in the company's order, in the menu of every member it is shared with. Featuring a site shares it with no one.

[Member sites in the account menu](/member-sites/sites-home) covers the menu's groups, where `/accounts` opens and the limits. For these operations, see [Pin a site to the member's menu](/api-reference/site-pins/pin-a-site-to-the-members-menu), [Choose the member's home site](/api-reference/site-home/choose-the-members-home-site) and [Feature a site](/api-reference/member-site-features/feature-a-site).

## Declare what a site's scripts use

A site's JavaScript reaches Fluid with `window.fluid.fetch`, which behaves like `fetch`, and reads the signed-in viewer's own data that way: the owner's, or a share holder's. Each path it calls needs a scope, such as `profile: read`. A site lists the scopes its scripts use in one file, `config/access.json`. The file is published with the site, and `fluid member-site lint` checks your scripts against it. Once a site has published a declaration, Fluid refuses a call whose scope the file lacks, and the code reaches only what the file lists. A share holder gets their own data for those scopes, minus a few that deliver outside Fluid or reach other people, and never the owner's. See [Declare what a member site uses](/member-sites/access-declarations).

## Give a site a database

A member's own site can have a Postgres database that only its owner, and the owner's own site script, can use. The company's hosting pays for it, so ask the company before you turn one on. A site holds one, and a company holds at most 20. The `fluid member-site database` commands turn it on and off, create its tables and read its rows, and the site's script reads and writes rows with `fluid.fetch`. See [Give a site a database](/member-sites/database).

## Build a site

You build a member site in one of two ways. Both write the same files and pages, so you can use them together.

  </Card>
  <Card title="The member and company APIs" icon="code" href="/member-sites/apis">
    Create sites, write their files and pages, publish, share and copy them from your own code.
  </Card>

The member API at `/api/member/v2026-10/sites` builds a member's own sites with the member's credential. The company API at `/api/company/v2026-10/member-sites` oversees every site in the company and builds the company's sites with a company admin's credential. [Build member sites with the APIs](/member-sites/apis) covers the credentials and links every operation. Start with [Add a site](/api-reference/sites/add-a-site) for members and [Create a company site](/api-reference/member-sites/create-a-company-site) for admins.
