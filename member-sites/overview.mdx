A member site is a small site that Fluid serves to your members inside your company's own domain. Each one has its own layouts, pages, sections, snippets, text assets and menus, and it is owned by the company or by one of its members. Switching or publishing your storefront theme never changes a site.

<Note>
  Member sites must be turned on for your company by Fluid. To ask for them, [contact support](/help/getting-help). This page is about sites that a member or the company owns and builds. For the pages that a theme's member storefront groups into sites, see [Member sites in the member storefront](/themes/member-storefront/member-sites).
</Note>

## Know the building blocks

| Term | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Site | A named set of files and pages with a `handle`, a `visibility` and a `status` |
| Owner | The company or one member. The owner builds the site |
| Handle | The site's name in its URL: lowercase letters, digits and single hyphens, at most 40 characters |
| Page | One address in the site. Its content is a file, `pages/<slug>.liquid` |

A member can own at most 25 sites. A company has no limit, and an admin can set two fields on a company site that a member can't: `required`, whether members must have the site, and `position`, where it sits among the company's sites.

## Open a site

A signed-in member opens a page at `/accounts/<handle>/<page-slug>` on your company's domain. The home page is `/accounts/<handle>`.

A site's pages run in a sandboxed frame inside a page that holds the member's login. The frame can't use that login itself. Your site's JavaScript gets the viewer's data through `window.fluid`: see [Site SDK](/member-sites/site-sdk).

### Private and public

A site's `visibility` is `private` or `public`, and a new site is private. It decides who can open the site once it is published:

- **Private.** The owner, company admins and the members a share matches can open it. A signed-in member who can't open it gets `404`.
- **Public.** Anyone with the link can open it, signed in or not. A guest sees the page with no `member`.

### Draft, published and taken down

A site's `status` is `draft`, `published` or `taken_down`. A new site is a draft. Layouts, sections, snippets and pages are saved as drafts, and publishing makes them live. A company admin can take down any site, and its owner can't publish it again.

## Share and copy

An owner shares a site with a named member, the owner's downline, or a segment of the company's members. Each share grants `view` or `copy` permission. A member with `copy` permission can make a private draft copy of a published site and edit it as their own. See [Share a site](/api-reference/site-shares/share-a-site).

## Lay out the files

A site's files use theme-style paths with no subfolders. `fluid member-site pull` writes them to a folder in this layout:

```text
rileys-shop/
  layouts/site.liquid     sections/hero.liquid     snippets/card.liquid
  pages/index.liquid      pages/about.liquid
  assets/site.css         assets/site.js
  config/settings.json    config/menus.json        config/pages.json
  .member-site.json
```

- `layouts/site.liquid` is the site's default layout. A page can name another layout, or `none` for no layout.
- `pages/index.liquid` is the home page.
- `config/pages.json` holds the site's default layout and, for each page, its slug, title, position, `published` flag, layout and menu.
- `config/settings.json` holds the site's settings, such as colors and fonts.
- `config/menus.json` holds the site's menus.
- A site holds text assets only: css, js, json, txt or html. Use images and fonts from the media library by URL.
- `.member-site.json` is the CLI's local record of where the folder came from. It is never pushed.

A layout prints the page with `{{ content_for_layout }}` and prints `{{ content_for_header }}` in its `<head>`. The header holds the link to `assets/site.css` and loads the SDK.

## Read the `site` and `member` objects

A page's Liquid reads these variables:

| Variable | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `site.name`, `site.handle` | The site's name and handle |
| `site.url` | The site's home URL, `/accounts/<handle>` |
| `site.pages` | The published pages in position order. Each has `title`, `slug`, `url`, `home` and `current` |
| `site.pages_menu` | The same pages as a menu with `title`, `handle` and `menu_items` |
| `site.menus` | The site's menus by slug, such as `site.menus.footer` |
| `page` | The page being rendered, with the same fields as an entry of `site.pages` |
| `settings` | The site's published settings, plus `settings.menu` |
| `member` | The signed-in viewer, or nothing for a guest |

`settings.menu` is the menu assigned to the page, else the site's `main` menu, else `site.pages_menu`. A menu and its items have a `title`, a `url` and `sub_menu_items`, the same shape as a storefront menu, so a navigation snippet written for a theme renders it unchanged.

```liquid
{% for item in site.pages %}
  <a href="{{ item.url }}" {% if item.current %}aria-current="page"{% endif %}>{{ item.title }}</a>
{% endfor %}
```

Every `url` is the address in the browser, not an internal one. A page that isn't published isn't listed.

### What `member` holds

A page can run code that its owner wrote, so `member` holds only `first_name` and `avatar_url`. The one exception is the member who owns the site and views it: they see their full profile, as in [Member variables](/themes/member-storefront/member-variables#member-profile). Every viewer of a company site gets the two public fields.

## Build a site

- **With the CLI.** Pull a site to a folder, edit it, lint it, push the changes and publish. See the [`fluid member-site` reference](/member-sites/cli-reference).
- **With the API.** Create a site, write its files, add pages and publish with the member API or the company API. See [Build a site with the API](/member-sites/build-with-the-api).
- **With JavaScript in the site.** Read and act for the viewer with [Site SDK](/member-sites/site-sdk).
